AboutThe story behind this work

This is for you brother.

A toolkit built with Black men, for Black men. From Lambeth, to everywhere.

Welcome. Take a breath. You have opened something that was built with you in mind.

This toolkit is a gift from Lambeth, South London to Black men everywhere, but it started close to home. It is the work of Black Thrive Lambeth, a partnership founded in 2016 between Lambeth Council, South London and Maudsley NHS Foundation Trust, the old Lambeth Clinical Commissioning Group, and a coalition of community organisations who were tired of seeing the same story on repeat: Black men failed by the very systems that were supposed to care for them.

Much of that energy carries the memory of Sean Rigg, a 40-year-old Black man and talented musician who died in Brixton police custody in August 2008 after a mental-health crisis. His family's long fight for truth is one of the reasons this work exists at all.

This version of the toolkit was designed in partnership with The Office of Art and Technology, a design studio that builds digital tools with communities, not just for them. We have spent hours listening to Black men across the country via 10-minute in-person conversations, focus groups and interviews: all of it shaped what you are about to read.

Here is what we want you to know.

We see you. We know the weight you carry. We know what it feels like to be told from boyhood to "man up," to hold it down for family, to be the rock while quietly cracking. We know the side-eye you get at the GP, the letters from the bank that pile up in the drawer, the sleepless nights you never talk about. We know the stories of strong fathers, grandfathers, and uncles who gave everything and still left this world tired. We know the reluctance to ask for help, because asking has sometimes cost us dearly.

This toolkit will not lecture you. It will not pretend racism does not exist or that one walk a day fixes everything. What it will do is give you six honest conversations, about your body, your mind, your money, your spirit, your brothers, and your community. Each one is anchored in a story of a Black man you might recognise.

A note on the men in this toolkit. The six characters you will meet are composites, drawn from many conversations with real Black men. They are aged between 24 and 65, the years when most of us are carrying the heaviest weight: working, parenting, building, sometimes burying. We have not given them specific ages on purpose. Wherever you sit in that range, one of these stories is yours.

Use this at your pace. Skip around. Read one section, then close the page and come back next week. Share it with a brother, a cousin, a son. Faith matters to many of us, and where it is useful we will name it; where it is not your path, the advice still stands. There is no right way to use this, except honestly.

You deserve a long life, a clear mind, a stable future, a known soul, real brothers, and a community you helped build. Let's get into it.
